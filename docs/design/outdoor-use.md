# Outdoor Use Design Note

ARC Probe is primarily conceived as an indoor product: a small environmental probe for plants, classrooms, studios, and other lived-in spaces where air quality, light, humidity, and plant care can be observed over time.

That said, the current shell direction also keeps an outdoor path open.

## Sealed Shell Direction

The public concept render shows a compact hexagonal body with a closed top and no exposed USB opening. In that direction, the outer shell can be treated more like a continuous plastic container than a perforated electronics enclosure.

If the shell is properly sealed, a few drops of water resting on the flat top are not necessarily a problem in themselves. A simple plastic bottle can hold water reliably because the material and closure strategy do the work. ARC Probe would need the same kind of thinking: the right plastic, controlled wall thickness, reliable joints, and a careful sealing strategy.

This note does not claim a tested waterproof rating. It records the design intent: avoid unnecessary openings, keep the exterior simple, and make the enclosure direction compatible with future water-resistance work.

## Material Direction

The material choice matters as much as the shape.

A semi-transparent plastic is attractive because it could allow the internal light sensor and status LED to function without obvious windows or extra openings. That would support a cleaner shell and reduce visual complexity.

The exact appearance is still open. Possible directions include:

- A smoky or frosted translucent material.
- A muted green material connected to plants and classrooms.
- A more opaque shell with carefully placed optical zones if needed.

The final choice should balance optical performance, UV behavior, surface finish, moldability, long-term aging, and the calm visual language of the product.

## Electronics Protection

Outdoor use would require more than a sealed-looking shell.

If ARC Probe is used outside, the electronics should be protected against condensation, moisture ingress, and environmental stress. Conformal coating is a plausible part of that strategy, especially for the PCB and sensitive exposed conductors.

A future outdoor-capable version would need validation of:

- Enclosure sealing and water paths.
- Material behavior under sunlight, temperature shifts, and humidity.
- Optical transmission for the light sensor and LED.
- Battery safety and charging behavior across expected conditions.
- Long-term corrosion resistance.
- Real-world rain, splash, condensation, and drying cycles.

## Product Boundary

The main ARC Probe concept remains indoor-first. The product is being developed for plants, classrooms, and learning environments where the probe can make indoor microclimate visible.

But if the shell, material, and electronics protection are developed carefully, the same design direction could theoretically support outdoor experiments as well. The flat-top hexagonal body is helpful here because it is simple, closed, easy to reason about mechanically, and compatible with a clean molded enclosure.
